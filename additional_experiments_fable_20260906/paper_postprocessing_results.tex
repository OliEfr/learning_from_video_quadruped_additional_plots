\textbf{Keypoint noise and post-processing.} Figure~7 compares the reconstructed keypoint trajectories of the video pipeline with the MoCap data at the same $30\,$Hz sampling rate. Before filtering, the reconstructed keypoints scatter about their true position by $0.7\%$ (torso) and $2.4\%$ (paws) of the hip height during ground contact, $2.6\times$ and $2.0\times$ the values of the MoCap data. Only $0.4\%$ of the depth samples are rejected as outliers, whereas $27\%$ of the paw samples ($10$--$49\%$ per clip) are interpolated because of occlusions or missing depth readings. The zero-phase low-pass reduces the high-frequency residual of the paw keypoints from $12.8\,$mm to $3.4\,$mm and the spectral power above $5\,$Hz by a factor of six, while the motion itself is preserved: net displacement and stride frequency are unchanged, the stance fraction changes from $44\%$ to $42\%$, and the peak paw clearance decreases by $6\%$. The remaining error is low-frequency, i.e. a scatter of the stance height ($2\%$ of hip height) and of the torso length ($8\%$), which no temporal filter removes; the policies nevertheless learn from this data as well as from MoCap (Figure~4).
