% (a) short:
Figure~8 makes the link between data coverage and policy performance explicit: for every target command, the tracking error grows with the distance of the command to the nearest expert frame (rank correlation $\rho = 0.61$ for MoCap and $0.31$ for Video (extended)), and the states visited by the agent move away from the expert data at the same time ($\rho = 0.38$ and $0.40$ between tracking error and expert imitation). The policy cannot imitate what the data does not contain, and where it cannot imitate, task tracking suffers as well.
% (b) with the AMP explanation (use if variant (b) of Fig. 5 was NOT used, otherwise the discriminator argument is already made):
Figure~8 explains why coverage matters for AMP. The discriminator sees only joint positions and velocities, so a target command without expert support forces the agent into transitions that are penalised by the style reward. Accordingly, the tracking error of every target command grows with its distance to the nearest expert frame ($\rho = 0.61$ MoCap, $0.31$ Video (extended)), and it is positively correlated with the distance between the agent's joint states and the expert data ($\rho = 0.38$ and $0.40$): far from the data, the policy neither imitates nor tracks well.
