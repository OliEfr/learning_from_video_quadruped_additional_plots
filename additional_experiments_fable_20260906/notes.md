# Notes for the authors - findings that contradict or complicate the paper, and pipeline bugs

Ordered by severity. Each item: evidence (file:line), consequence for the paper, suggested fix.

## 1. `3d_recon.py` back-projects 640x360 pixel tracks with the 1280x720 intrinsics (major)
* Evidence: `camera_info/camera.yaml` intrinsics fx=748.1, fy=747.5, cx=634.5, cy=370.8 are those of the 1280x720 colour image (principal point at the image centre of 1280x720). The 2D tracks (`tracks/*/foot_pos.npy`, `hip_pos.npy`, int32) and the depth maps (`in/*/depth_cam/*.raw`, reshaped to 360x640 in `3d_recon.py:229-230`) are at half resolution (max track x = 604, max y = 357). `3d_recon.py:209-212` builds the Open3D intrinsics with `width=1280, height=720` and `3d_recon.py:316-317` computes `X = (u - CX) * z / FX` with the full-resolution FX/CX on half-resolution `u`.
* Effect: `X_code = 0.5*X_true - 0.42*z`, `Y_code = 0.5*Y_true - 0.25*z` (lateral coordinates halved plus a depth-dependent shear); depth is unaffected. The reconstructed dog has a hip height of 0.16-0.24 m in the pipeline output; re-lifting the same tracks/depth/poses with fx/2, cx/2 gives 0.38-0.52 m (`data/relift_validation.json`), and a single-frame check (`slow` clip, frame 20: hip-to-paw 92 px at 1.5 m) gives 0.19 m vs. 0.37 m. The RGB frame shows a medium-sized dog (knee height of the adult next to it), so the corrected value is the plausible one. Our re-implementation with the as-coded intrinsics reproduces the on-disk keypoints exactly (median inter-marker distance difference 0.00 mm).
* Consequences: (a) base velocities in the video expert files are underestimated by up to 2x for image-plane motion (Exp. 2 numbers use the dataset values because that is what the policies imitated); (b) the retargeting hides the error behind per-clip constants (REF_POS_SCALE 1.0 for the small "video dog" vs. 0.825 for MoCap, SIM_ROOT_OFFSET +0.08 m, 12 foot-offset numbers per clip - see Exp. 3); (c) heading/yaw derived from the two torso keypoints is distorted direction-dependently, which likely explains the 20-40 % torso-segment length variation and the +-4 rad/s yaw-rate noise in the turning clips (Exp. 1/2); (d) noise numbers in the lateral directions are lower bounds.
* Fix: in `3d_recon.py` use the intrinsics of the undistorted half-size image (`newcameramtx` from `image_processing.py:93-96` scaled by 0.5, or simply fx/2, fy/2, cx/2, cy/2) both for the point cloud and for the marker back-projection; then re-run retargeting with REF_POS_SCALE ~0.6-0.7 and re-tune (ideally remove) the per-clip offsets. Until then, state in the paper that the reconstruction is metrically inconsistent in the image plane.

## 2. Paw keypoints were annotated by hand per frame, not tracked (major for the claims in Sec. III-A and V)
* Evidence: `quadruped_from_video/README.md` step 4: "Currently using the notebook for frame-wise manual foot pos annotation (base pos can be tracked automatically quite well)"; `co-tracker/notebooks/manual_tracking.ipynb` cell 11: "Select points for frame {frame}. Click order: HR, HL, VR, VL. Select top right corner for undefined points."; tracks are integer pixel coordinates; `walk` even has two hand-made variants (`foot_pos_real.npy` and `foot_pos_for_depth_cam.npy`, the latter displaced by 4 px on average, up to 20 px, to pixels with valid depth - `3d_recon.py:56-63`).
* The paper says "we continuously track sparse keypoints ... using a TAP model, e.g., co-tracker" and in Sec. V "instead of manually selecting the keypoints" (which reads as one click per keypoint). A reviewer who reads the repository will see this. Roughly 4 paws x 366 frames ~ 1500 clicks were needed for the flat set alone; this is excluded from Exp. 3 by instruction but must be disclosed.
* Fix: either (a) run CoTracker on the paws for the released clips and report its error against the manual annotation (a real "tracking noise" experiment), or (b) reword Sec. III-A: torso keypoints tracked with CoTracker, paw keypoints annotated per frame because TAP tracking of paws was not reliable (README: "Tracking w/ masks is a bit better, but still not sufficient").

## 3. The depth post-processing described in Sec. III-A is not in the code
* Paper: "discarding depth values that differ by more than 0.5 m from the previous value ... linear interpolation ... moving average filter over the last three frames". On disk there is only column-wise linear interpolation of zero entries with linear **extrapolation** at the clip borders (`3d_recon.py:301-312`, `fill_value="extrapolate"`) plus hand-typed per-clip deletions (`3d_recon.py:280-296`). No 0.5 m jump test and no moving average exist in `3d_recon.py`, `postprocess_global_align_plane_approach.py` or `retarget_motion_fromVision.py`.
* Consequence: the noise measured in Exp. 1 is the noise of the unfiltered pipeline; the extrapolation produces outliers when a paw is missing at a clip border (re-lifting `turn_right` with a different trimming changes some inter-marker distances by up to 208 mm, `data/relift_validation.json`).
* Fix: implement the described filter (or delete the sentence) and replace `fill_value="extrapolate"` by nearest-value padding. On average 18 % of paw samples have zero depth at the tracked pixel (47 % in `walk`), so this filter matters.
* Follow-up (see results.md, "Filter proposal"): evaluated on the clips, the described 0.5 m jump rule would fire only twice in 3730 depth samples, and a robust Hampel outlier stage flags 0.4 % - i.e. depth *outliers* are not the problem; the white noise above ~6 Hz is removable with a zero-phase low-pass (paw residual 9.3 -> 2.5 mm, motion preserved), but the low-frequency torso-segment inconsistency (21 %) and stance-height scatter are not filterable. Prefer a zero-phase filter over the causal 3-frame moving average in the text (which shifts contact timing by one frame).

## 4. Angular velocities are not computed
* Paper: "We then compute the joint velocities, base linear velocities, and angular velocities using finite differences." Columns 34:37 (angular velocity) are zero in every expert file (`checks_report.md`); `output_motion_amp()` never fills them. Harmless for AMP (the discriminator uses joint pos/vel only) but RSI initialises a zero yaw rate even in turning clips. Fix: fill columns 34:37 from the quaternion differences, or drop the sentence.

## 5. The base yaw of the video clips is very noisy; the turning clips exceed the command range
* From Exp. 1/2: the torso-segment length varies by 27-38 % within the turning clips; the raw yaw rate has a noise std of 0.4-0.8 rad/s (MoCap 0.02); even after 0.23 s smoothing the p5-p95 range is [0.05, 4.44] rad/s for `turn_left` whose net rotation is 143 deg in 1.47 s (1.7 rad/s mean). `turn_left/right` and `slow_turn` all turn at ~1.7 rad/s mean, above the 1.57 rad/s command limit; `left_right_turn` swings between -3.9 and +5.6 rad/s with a net change of -18 deg.
* The paper attributes MoCap's yaw error to missing turning data (Fig. 7); the video sets do cover more yaw-rate cells (Exp. 2), but their yaw signal is partly noise. Consider: heading from a smoothed torso trajectory or from more torso points; low-pass the yaw before differencing; report the noise.

## 6. Wrong citation for the MoCap duration
* Sec. IV, Video recordings: "a total duration of 6.0 s similar to data in the MoCap dataset [44]". [44] is Y. Zhang et al., "Efficient entropy for policy gradient ...". The dataset is [7] (H. Zhang et al., 2018). (The MoCap expert set on disk is 6.80 s at FrameDuration 0.021 s, i.e. 5.5 s of real dog motion, see item 7.)

## 7. MoCap is replayed 26 % slower than recorded
* The raw Zhang et al. clips are 60 Hz (`data/dog_clips_info.txt`: frames/seconds = 59.7); `retarget_motion.py:42` uses FRAME_DURATION = 0.021 s (inherited from AMP_for_hardware; the comment there notes the original 0.01667). All MoCap expert velocities (Exp. 2, RSI, the 6.80 s duration) are therefore 0.79x the real dog's. Video is replayed in real time (0.03334 s). This is a fairness caveat for every MoCap-vs-video velocity statement; mention it or use 1/60 s.

## 8. Reproducibility of the expert files
* `rsl_rl/.../motion_loader.py:254-259` now asserts FrameDuration == 0.06 for any file whose path contains "vision"; the paper's flat-walking sets have 0.03334 and can no longer be loaded with the current loader. The later sets (`*_feetZAmpl_minimal*`, stairs `*_feet_forward`, box, stand-up) are at 0.06 s = half speed, so "Video" means different replay speeds in different scenarios. Pin the loader/dataset pairing in the release, or make FrameDuration a dataset property without an assert.
* Intermediate artefacts are stale for some clips: `3d_recon/*/depth_cam/markers.npy` and `postprocess/*/depth_cam/*.npy` have different frame counts from the retargeting inputs (`walk` 25 vs 27, `slow` 26 vs 67, ...). The trimming (`start_idx_files`, `end_idx_files` in `3d_recon.py:13-14`) is set by hand per run and not recorded. Move all per-clip settings (trimming, deletions, offsets) into one YAML per clip; this also shrinks the hand-coding counted in Exp. 3 and makes it auditable.

## 9. AMP sampling makes short clips dominant
* `AMPLoader` samples a trajectory with probability MotionWeight/sum and then a time uniformly within it, so the per-frame density is proportional to weight/length. In the MoCap set the 29-frame `canter` (2.1 m/s) receives the same mass as the 150-frame `right turn0`; 45 % of the MoCap expert mass is faster than the 1 m/s command limit. In the extended video set `walk` has MotionWeight 2. Worth a sentence when discussing why MoCap policies track fast commands but not slow/standing ones.

## 9b. Joint (leg) order of the expert files - three different conventions in the code, please verify
* The 12 joint columns of the `*_amp.txt` files come straight from `pybullet.calculateInverseKinematics2`, i.e. in URDF joint order, which for `assets/go2/go2.urdf` is **FL, FR, RL, RR** (hip, thigh, calf each). Independent check: forward kinematics of the MoCap `trot2` expert file reproduces the saved reference toe targets (`data_foot_pos/trot2_ref_toe_pos_all.npy`) with a 13 cm residual under this order (expected magnitude of the retargeting toe offsets and scale), vs 19 cm for FL,FR,RR,RL and 28 cm for FR,FL,RR,RL.
* `retarget_config_go2.py` `DEFAULT_JOINT_POSE` (-0.1, 0.8, -1.5 | 0.1, 0.8, -1.5 | -0.1, 1.0, -1.5 | 0.1, 1.0, -1.5) is commented "reordered to mujoco order" = FR, FL, RR, RL; under the URDF order it means hips -0.1 for FL/RL and +0.1 for FR/RR, the opposite of the IsaacLab default (L +0.1, R -0.1). This only sets IK rest poses, so it is not critical.
* `source/constants.yaml` `JOINT_UNITREE_TO_ISAAC_LAB_MAPPING = [0, 3, 9, 6, ...]` (used by `motion_loader.reorder_from_pybullet_to_isaac_lab`) maps IsaacLab RL_hip <- column 9 and RR_hip <- column 6. Under the URDF order column 9 is **RR** and column 6 is **RL**, i.e. the rear legs would be swapped when the expert data enter Isaac Lab (the FK test above shows exactly this swap doubles the rear-foot residual). The inverse constant `JOINT_ISAAC_LAB_TO_UNITREE_MAPPING` ("VERIFIED IN SIMULATION") is *not* the inverse of the first one - they differ by an FL/FR swap - so at least one of the two is wrong. If the loader mapping is the wrong one, the discriminator learned rear-leg-mirrored gaits (for a trot this flips the diagonal pairing). Suggested check: load one expert file in Isaac Lab, set the robot to frame 0 and compare the paw positions with the pybullet retargeting visualisation.

## 9c. "Imitation score" (agent_expert_distances) is a joint-velocity distance and favours standing still
* `play.py:469` computes `torch.cdist(amp_observations, interpolated_expert_trajectories).min(dim=1)` on the raw (q, qdot) vector. Joint velocities are O(5-15 rad/s), joint angles O(0.5 rad), so 94-96 % of the squared nearest-neighbour distance comes from the 12 velocity dimensions (`data/exp4_amp_evidence.json`, `calibration.qdot_share`). A standing Go2 (qdot = 0, default pose from constants.yaml) is at distance 1.9 from the MoCap set although MoCap contains no standing frame (its slowest frame has ||qdot|| = 1.8 rad/s); the MoCap policy's best "imitation" cell in Fig. 5 is the standing command. Rename to "agent-expert distance" or normalise per dimension (e.g. by the expert std, as the AMP normalizer does for the discriminator) before calling it an imitation score.
* The same per-frame velocity dominance also means the discriminator's expert distribution is, to first order, a distribution over joint *speeds*; for MoCap these encode base speed well (rho 0.68) but for the video sets only weakly (0.22-0.27) because the retargeted joint velocities are noise-dominated (Exp. 1). Filtering the keypoints (Exp. 1 filter proposal) would therefore also sharpen what the discriminator learns.
* AMP sampling (extends item 9): in both MoCap and Video (extended) the clip with the highest per-frame sampling density (canter 5.2x, walk 5.4x the lowest) is the one clip outside the 1 m/s command range; 45 % / 22 % of the expert mass is faster than any command the policy is evaluated on. `MotionWeight` is the obvious knob and is currently 1.0 everywhere except walk = 2.

## 10. Smaller items
* Dead branch: `retarget_config_go2.py:219` tests `"obstacle_1_301506103500"` (typo, extra "35"); the real clip is handled at line 235. Harmless for the paper.
* Video expert quaternions have zero x-component (no roll) - consistent with the paper; MoCap has roll. Fine, but the two sets differ in that state dimension.
* `plot_DEFINITIONS.get_color_for_experiment_name` uses `plt.cm.get_cmap`, removed in matplotlib >= 3.9 (the isaaclab env has 3.9.2); the figure scripts of the paper will crash there. Our scripts reproduce the same tab10 indices.
* The claim "The translation is such that the robot's base starts at (0, 0, robot height)" (Sec. III-A): `postprocess_global_align_plane_approach.py:110-112` puts the *rear-hip marker of frame 0* at the origin (z = 0); the robot height comes only from the retargeting offsets (SIM_ROOT_OFFSET 0.08 m + per-clip local offsets).
* The mask files named `*_1280x720.png` are actually 640x360 (fine for the code, confusing for readers).

## What can and cannot be claimed from this data
* Can: relative noise ratios between the two sources at matched 30 Hz and normalised size (Exp. 1); support/coverage of the command distribution by each expert set and which cells the extension adds (Exp. 2); the amount and kind of per-clip hand-coding in the scripts (Exp. 3).
* Cannot: absolute tracking accuracy of the video pipeline (no ground truth; MoCap's own noise is unknown, so ratios are conservative upper bounds on how much *worse* video is only if MoCap is treated as noise-free); true dog velocities from the video sets (item 1); the manual annotation effort in clicks or hours (out of scope by instruction, but see item 2); any policy-level effect beyond what can be read from the shipped Fig. 5 PDF (Exp. 4 uses its recovered, colour-clipped cell values) - the evaluation logs are not on this machine.
